# Recon capture components

This is a **proposed static design contract for later Android implementation**, paired with the 1440 × 9000 canonical `recon-capture-design-system.svg`. It is not evidence that the existing Kotlin implements these states. Mode symbols now use the `modeStrip*` names; all generated consumers are rebuilt from shared definitions.

## Geometry and queue families

All interactive specimens are at 1:1 dp-like units. Only camera-scene comparisons are reduced and labeled. Do not uniformly scale the screen or touch targets. Use the explicit dense sizes below for short bottom-dock windows; text stays readable at its existing sizes. Toolbar glyph boxes are 24dp inside allocated 48 × 48dp targets. Mode uses solid white bold text for selection and gray medium-weight text for other choices in a 250 × 40dp row, with three independent 83⅓ × 48dp targets. Zoom uses a 28dp selected circle within a 32dp-thick dark surface, 12sp text matching modality labels, independent 48 × 48dp targets and 48dp center pitch. Thumbnail media markers are decorative and are not separate controls.

| Component | Contract |

| Shutter, every mode | Standard 80 × 80, ring radius 38.5; dense 64 × 64, ring radius 30.5. Both white stroke 3; explicit geometry, not scaled strokes |
| Photo / video idle / voice idle center | 64dp white / 42dp red / 58dp white |
| Recording Stop | 28 × 28 red square, 6dp corners; white ring unchanged; remains enabled |
| Voice idle microphone | Proposed 32dp glyph box; native uses 24dp; retain official proportions |
| Full queue | 360 × 72; thumbnails x59, 121, 183, 245, y8; all 56 × 56 |
| Full edge targets | x0 and x312, y12; each 48 × 48; centers x24 and x336; 6 × 28 handles x21 and x333, y22 |
| Full split | Thumbnails x50, 112, 192, 254; expanded gap x168–192; divider x178, width 4 |
| Compact queue | `queueCompact`, 312 × 72; thumbnails x66, 128, 190, y8; all 56 × 56 |
| Compact edge targets | x0 and x264, y12; each 48 × 48; centers x24 and x288; 6 × 28 handles x21 and x285, y22 |

For a bottom dock, `queueTop = windowHeight − safeBottom − 24 − queueHeight`. Coordinates include the full window; if the layout already uses inset content bounds, do not subtract the inset again. `safeBottom` is the applicable union/max of drawing and gesture insets, never their sum. The 24dp shown in ordinary fixtures is illustrative. Physical-device insets may change this number.

Use the **dense profile** when the bottom-dock usable height (`windowHeight − safeTop − safeBottom`) is below 600dp; this is a provisional design breakpoint. Freeze the selected profile and geometry during a pointer sequence, or cancel without mutation before relayout. Dense geometry is explicit: shutter 64, photo center 50, video center 34, voice disk 48/mic 28, stop 24 with 5dp corners; queue 312 × 64, thumbnails 48, vertical padding 8, handles 48 × 48 at x0/264,y8. Visible handles stay 6 ×28, centered at y32. Durations remain 11sp; mode/zoom remain 12sp. Standard queue geometry above remains the reference for taller windows.

Dense short queues use x78/132/186; dense split uses x69/123/195 and divider x181 within the 24dp expanded gap. Dense overflow uses 48dp tiles and 54dp pitch, endpoint padding 56, the same horizontal alpha mask as compact standard, and 64dp height. Handles retain their dimensions, priority and horizontal anchors; vertical positions recenter within the shorter row. Visible media underneath still cannot steal a handle start.

Queue/shutter gap is 12dp standard and 8dp dense. Zoom target geometry stays 48dp, with its visible viewport 52dp above the shutter top. Ordinary 360 × 800 phone: queue(24,680), shutter y588. Cover 360 × 576: queue(24,464), shutter y392; its visible tile bottoms are y520, leaving 24dp actual gesture corridor plus 8dp row padding plus 24dp illustrative system area. The dense profile reduces visual weight and spacing without shrinking text or interactive targets below 48dp.

Controls may overlay the preview. The revised cover composition uses a full-width 360 × 480 preview at y72, with controls over a bottom contrast gradient; this contract does not require all controls to sit outside camera content. Overlay controls and queue gestures have input priority over preview focus, pinch and mode swipe wherever their regions intersect. Preserve the selected 72/64dp strip,24dp downward corridor and actual bottom safety, and keep overlay controls legible against changing camera content. A control gesture must not also operate the preview underneath.

Both queues have **no background bar**. Compact Commit/Discard variants replace only the initiating handle with the corresponding colored swiper, retain the opposite handle, and fade inward in the direction of travel. Full variants retain their larger fade regions but use the same fixed edge targets and centered glyph slots. The compact split has a 6dp first gap and a 24dp second gap; all three items remain fully visible.

Three visible items describe the compact **short-queue** composition, not a queue limit. Short queues remain centered. Long queues scroll horizontally in chronological order, with partial thumbnails extending under stationary handles. `queueLong{Start,Middle,End,Split,Commit,Discard}` (360dp) and `queueCompactLong…` (312dp) are 1:1 reference states; `queueCompactOverflow` aliases the compact middle state.

- Apply an **alpha mask to media and dividers only**, not a black overlay: transparent from the outer edge to 24dp, ramp to fully visible at 56dp; mirror at the right edge. Nothing paints a queue background bar. Keep handles/swipers above this masked layer and outside its mask.
- At the start, the first tile begins at x56. At the end, the final tile ends at x=`width − 56`. These 8dp clearances beyond the edge hit zones make both endpoint tiles fully reachable. The six-item examples have 366dp standard / 318dp dense media width, each plus 112dp endpoint padding. A split adds 18dp to replace a 6dp gap with 24dp; the 4dp divider is centered in that gap and scrolls with it.
- Start shows fading continuation on the right; middle on both sides; end on the left. Endpoints have no hidden content on their finished side. Standard thumbnails remain 56dp; the dense family uses 48dp. Ordinary gaps remain 6dp. No permanent overflow count or arrows appear inside the app.
- **Input priority does not follow opacity.** Standard edge targets remain `[0,48) × [12,60)` and `[width−48,width) × [12,60)`; dense targets use the same x ranges and y`[8,56)`. Any initial contact in either target belongs exclusively to that handle, even over a visible tile or divider. Subtract these rectangles from effective item/gap hit regions and accessibility hit bounds. Do not enlarge targets to the full strip height. Handle taps retain their existing behavior; the swipe thresholds are unchanged.
- Resolve the initial owner before recognizing scroll, long-press reorder, delete or split. A tray-owned gesture stays tray-owned when it crosses an edge zone; a handle-owned gesture never scrolls or selects media. Freeze tray scrolling during a handle swipe. Commit/discard replaces only that handle; the opposite remains visible. Queue actions still affect the whole queue, not only visible items.
- Fully obscured/offscreen items must not expose overlapping on-screen accessibility targets. Scroll them into the central region before interaction; preserve access to all items through normal accessible list scrolling. Reorder auto-scroll may continue for an already-owned item without activating an edge action.

Empty queues keep subdued handles with no enabled queue actions; a single item is centered at x128 in the compact family. Screen examples for start/middle/end/split appear in the layout-check sheet over the actual cover preview; all explanatory labels stay outside app viewports.

## Mode and zoom placement and meaning

Keep VIDEO–PHOTO–VOICE horizontal, all three visible, centered in the top bar in every layout, including side docks and VOICE. Mode selection is less frequent; idle preview swipe is the quick path. Selection uses solid white text at weight 700; unselected labels use gray text at weight 500. No underline, dash or selected pill is drawn. Locked selection retains bold weight with its existing disabled-state colors. The 250 × 40dp row starts at x=`window width / 2 − 125`, y28; its three equal targets span y24–72. The design-system VIDEO and VOICE recording compositions likewise keep their locked mode rows at the top. Preserve shutter and queue anchors across mode and zoom changes; hiding zoom for VOICE must not shift them. Complete window states are in `recon-capture-recording-states.svg`; the generator verifies these anchors against the PHOTO fixtures.

Zoom is horizontal directly above the shutter on compact/bottom layouts and on side shells with wide frames: its symbol viewport starts 52dp above the shutter top, with the visible surface inset 4dp vertically. For side shells with tall frames, place a vertical rail opposite the shutter dock, centered on the fitted preview. Text stays upright; zoom values ascend bottom-to-top (.5, 1×, 2, 5 in the illustrated fixture). Move the rail to the right for a left-hand shutter dock. This rule depends on shell and upright frame shape, not window orientation alone. Four-choice visible surfaces are 176 × 32dp horizontally or 32 × 176dp vertically, within unscaled 192 × 40 / 40 × 192 symbol viewports. The 48 × 48dp targets touch at boundaries but never overlap. A single-choice surface is 32dp across with the same 28dp selected circle and 48dp target. All zoom labels, including continuous values, use 12sp; use text adaptation/expanded controls at large font settings rather than shrinking touch targets.

Display fractional shortcut labels without the leading zero (`.5`); the numeric zoom ratio remains 0.5 and accessibility descriptions should announce the full value.

Choices come from active-camera capabilities and supported zoom range, not a fixed set of optical lenses or a universal rear/front rule. The front single-preset 1× fixture assumes continuous zoom exists; hide the entire control, hit regions and semantics if no adjustment is available. Continuous zoom displays the actual value: the 1.7× specimen replaces the 1× slot, without marking a preset falsely active. Tapping that current-value slot resets to 1×; semantic output must convey “Current zoom 1.7×; reset to 1×” and expose the reset action, rather than promising selection of 1.7×.

Read exact screen bounds from `layout-geometry.json`, generated by `layout-screens.mjs`. Tall-frame right-dock PHOTO previews are 456 × 608 at (96,86) for Fold8 wide and (96,158) for Ultra wide; their shutter tops remain y484/y628 and queue tops y576/y720. The left-hand fixture preview is (408,86), 456 × 608, with zoom targets at x888; right-hand zoom targets are at x24. The near-square tall-frame fixture uses a bottom dock, with preview (208.5,84), 447 × 596 and queue (252,840); this yields more preview area than its prior side dock. Bottom-dock tall-window PHOTO previews are 672 × 504 at (24,130) for Fold8 and 794.6667 × 596 at (34.6667,84) for Ultra; both now use shutter y748, queue y840 and horizontal zoom targets at y692. The tall-window/tall-frame bottom-dock exception fits a 447 × 596 preview at (136.5,84). These are illustrative layout coordinates, not measured device metadata.

Where a compact preview extends behind the header, mode labels sit over a 72% black header scrim spanning y24–72. This keeps inactive `#BDBDBD` labels above 4.5:1 even over a white camera frame; do not judge contrast only against the illustrated test scene. Zoom has its own 72% black surface.

The supplied Samsung cover and iPhone cover/traditional references make zoom visually secondary to capture modes: compact numerals and small circular highlights. Their image pixels do not establish dp values. Recon therefore uses the same 12sp label size as its modes, reduces visual mass (220 × 40 to 176 × 32), and preserves independent 48dp targets. The same tokens apply to phone, cover and inner layouts; do not downscale the whole component on short screens. Preserve the common 1× target center across camera-capability changes: horizontal x=shutter center−24dp, vertical y=rail top+120dp. A singleton keeps that position instead of recentering.

## Tap-to-focus feedback

Current scope is tap-to-focus only. Draw a complete thin white ring with a transparent center: radius 30dp and stroke 2dp (62dp outer diameter) inside the existing 80 × 80 symbol viewport. This matches the existing Android `FocusIndicator` resting geometry in `CameraPreview.kt`. Remove the previous crosshair ticks. No padlock, ring gap for a lock icon, sun icon or exposure track is included.

The ring is feedback at the tapped camera-preview point, not a button or permanent idle decoration. Static preview examples show the moment after a focus tap; the ring alone does not assert autofocus success. Preserve the existing transient behavior as the implementation reference: scale 1.5→1 over 200ms, hold 500ms, fade over 200ms, and replace the marker on a new tap. These timing values come from current code, not the Samsung screenshot. Native edge clipping, transforms and animation remain runtime checks; target coordinates must stay tied to the actual tap.

Preview swipes and pinch gestures must not also trigger focus. Existing overlay controls retain gesture ownership. The gesture legend's small blue contact dot is an external instruction mark, not part of the in-app focus ring. Focus locking and exposure adjustment are explicitly outside this design update.

## Input, release and cancellation

The initial **hit-tested** zone owns a contact sequence until completion or cancellation. Fixed handle zones take priority over overflowing media beneath them. Outside those zones, thumbnail starts cannot subsequently be stolen by a divider lane or edge target. Do not expand a 6dp gap into neighboring thumbnail hit regions. One queue mutation at a time. Horizontal tray scrolling, downward deletion, long-press reorder and edge swipes must arbitrate before claiming the gesture. Once two pointers participate in preview zoom, the sequence cannot also focus, change mode or mutate the queue.

| Action | Proposed completion | Cancellation / reversal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Save queue: left target → right | Release after at least **96dp net inward displacement**, once | Below 96dp restores; velocity alone does not execute |
| Discard queue: right target → left | Same deliberate 96dp gate; then offer Undo | Same gate and restoration; no velocity-only destruction |
| Remove one item: thumbnail ↓ | Release only when net downward travel is **greater than 24dp** | Returning to ≤24dp disarms; pointer cancellation always restores |
| Split / join: visible gap ↓ / ↑ | Release after at least 24dp in the applicable direction; haptic on arming | Below threshold or cancellation restores prior split state |
| Reorder: thumbnail hold + horizontal drag | Release into a destination computed from actual item/gap geometry | Cancellation restores original order and split state |
| Preview swipe, idle | One adjacent mode on valid release: ≥64dp net travel, or ≥300dp/s release velocity agreeing with the net direction after horizontal intent wins | Below gate restores; no wrap at VIDEO/VOICE; cancellation never changes mode |

Preview swipe follows the existing `ModalitySwipeMath` distance/velocity values (64dp /300dp/s), with explicit input ownership: only one pointer, idle state, a start on exposed preview and horizontal dominance beyond platform touch slop. Evaluate **net** displacement at valid release; never accumulate outward-and-return travel. A velocity shortcut requires the release velocity to agree with nonzero net displacement. Reversal back below64dp cancels unless that same-direction flick gate is met; an opposite release velocity cannot trigger the flick gate. Distance-qualified releases follow net direction. From PHOTO, right→VIDEO and left→VOICE; from VIDEO only left→PHOTO; from VOICE only right→PHOTO. One step per gesture, no wrap and no move beyond the ends. Cancellation, a second pointer or recording transition restores the current mode, regardless of distance. Direct top-mode taps remain the accessible alternative. These values come from current code; physical feel and pointer cancellation need runtime validation.

The 96dp queue gate is a bounded **prototype proposal**, not a tuned hardware result. It is independent of window width and must be evaluated later. Distance is measured from the original contact, including reversal. An armed indicator clears when displacement falls below its gate. Ordinary pointer cancellation, an extra finger, loss of gesture ownership, lifecycle/window interruption, or departure from the safe corridor restores the original queue **even after arming**. Mutation happens only on an intentional valid release, once. Do not route cancellation through completion.

For deletion, use a proposed **>24dp net downward displacement plus valid release** on every profile; no velocity-only completion. Explicitly establish vertical intent before arming: after platform touch slop, the dominant axis owns the sequence, and ambiguous movement stays undecided. Once horizontal scrolling or long-press reorder owns it, subsequent downward drift cannot become deletion. Returning to ≤24dp disarms; extra pointers, cancellation and leaving the usable corridor restore the item. Check unsafe-bound cancellation before release completion. Provide the existing selected-item direct action as an alternative. This shortens the prior40dp proposal and must be tested for false triggers; it is not an Android-prescribed distance or an implemented change.

Clamp rendered travel to 32dp, independently tracking actual contact position. Progress is `clamp(dragY /24,0,1)`; alpha is `clamp(1 −0.6*dragY/32,0.4,1)`. The half specimen uses 12dp, alpha .775; armed uses 32dp, alpha .4. Red glow is painted before (behind) all media, fades from transparent at its upper edge, and stays entirely within the last12dp of the corridor, and has no shadow/stroke extending past the safe boundary. Standard latest thumbnail start y64 +32 =96 =72+24. Dense latest start y56 +32 =88 =64+24. The extra8dp beyond the 24dp arming boundary is available release headroom, not required release distance. Gap starts use only the thumbnail-height band (y8–64 standard, y8–56 dense); their 24dp split gesture also fits with headroom. Full-size translated media, feedback and contact paths fit above system UI; no clipping or gesture exclusion is used to manufacture clearance.
Reorder lifts the held item to 1.1×. The sheet shows the source outline and destination marker for review; temporary overlap with a neighboring thumbnail is expected while dragging. Do not calculate destinations using an invariant 62dp pitch across expanded gaps. Reordering may cross a split: splits remain boundaries between ordinal slots, while the item moves; do not silently merge or create splits. Cancel changes neither ordering nor boundaries. Overflow reorder can use bounded edge auto-scroll in the tray, without activating save/discard edge gestures. Move earlier/later remains the precise alternative.

## Accessible direct actions

Tapping a thumbnail opens the familiar `queueActionMenu` specimen. Its title identifies media type and position. All six action rows are 312 × 48dp: Remove item, Move earlier, Move later, Split after, Save queue, Discard queue. Replace Split after with Remove split where a following boundary exists; omit that row on the final item. Disable Move earlier/later at their respective ends. Save/Discard apply to the whole queue; discard offers Undo. Existing split boundaries partition saved bundles. Close the menu after an action and restore sensible focus; outside tap or Back closes it without mutation.

Expose the same commands as semantic/custom actions for TalkBack, Switch Access and Voice Access; no drag-only operation is required. Expose selected modality, zoom value, microphone state and unavailable controls accurately. Thumbnail descriptions must say Photo, Video or Voice rather than describing all media as photos. Decorative glyphs, waveform bars, tutorial illustrations and timer ticks must not create redundant focus stops or continual announcements.

This menu and the split semantic actions are **new design requirements**. Narrow gap gestures remain optional shortcuts and do not count as accessible 48dp tap targets. Relevant Android guidance: [touch targets and defaults](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/accessibility/api-defaults), [semantics and custom actions](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/accessibility/semantics).

## Recording and other control states

VIDEO shows a locked but readable selected mode, live preview, elapsed-time pill, audio status and enabled Stop. PHOTO has flash/lens controls; VIDEO has mic/lens controls; VOICE has neither. While recording, Settings, Library, mode, queue, mic and lens are locked. Disabling navigation avoids interrupting an active take by leaving capture; this is a proposed design requirement, not a claim about the existing native route handlers. Lock includes pointer detectors and accessibility actions, not just opacity; do not animate a successful exit for a rejected mutation. Camera zoom remains available during video recording, consistent with the native path; hide it and remove hit/semantic regions for VOICE. Unavailable hardware presets are omitted, not shown as working controls.

VOICE idle shows a flat waveform and “Tap shutter to record voice.” VOICE recording shows symmetric red amplitude bars, a red live dot, “Recording…” and elapsed time. Video's pill has audio-on, muted and long-duration examples. Preserve content width for growing elapsed time instead of scaling the text. Idle busy/capturing/rebinding shutters are disabled; active Stop remains enabled. Disabled specimens are visual references; implementation must expose disabled state and prevent activation.

Stop has a solid red square in this static contract; an optional native-style pulse may be retained only if its dimmest endpoint remains legible. Do not rely on animation or color alone to identify recording. Rotation continues the same take; fold/unfold finalizes once. These remain future implementation requirements, not runtime verification.

## Banners and large text

The 328 × 48 examples are **compact minimums**, not fixed heights or universal widths. Use the allocated overlay region minus its insets. Undo and Error action targets are respectively 72 × 48 and 64 × 48; the two-line example uses 72 × 48. Wrapped text has its own reserved area and never extends beneath the action. The standalone 2× text specimen stacks a 144 × 48 action below the message; text and targets retain actual sizes. It is a component reference, not proof that this banner fits every screen. Saved identifiers/ranges wrap as illustrated. Longer/localized content may grow further; preserve the complete message through wrapping or an accessible detail presentation, not silent clipping of actionable information.

The short-cover layout restores the 328 × 160 large-text error at (16,92), including its stacked 144 × 48dp action. It ends at y252; dense horizontal zoom targets begin at y336, giving 84dp clearance. Shutter y392 and queue y464 remain anchored. The separate 72dp multiline component specimen remains available, but is not the short-cover large-text fixture. This static composition does not establish runtime font-scaling acceptance. For larger or localized content that cannot fit, retain an alternate accessible presentation with complete text and persistently reachable actions while keeping Stop accessible.

An overlay host must keep the shutter/Stop and the queue's safe travel corridor accessible. Grow upward within a reserved safe host without moving capture controls. If large content cannot fit, use a bounded scrollable message region with its action persistently reachable; do not cover Stop or steal an active queue gesture. Device layout owns the available host geometry.

Only one feedback overlay is visible: **Error > Undo > Saved**. Retain a pending Undo while an error occupies the host; pause its timeout and resume when it becomes reachable. Announce a new message once through appropriate live-region semantics. Honor the user's accessibility-recommended timeout for actionable feedback; do not treat native's existing three-second Undo as proof of accessibility acceptance. Covered banners cannot remain invisible focus targets. Saved feedback does not need a dismiss action.

## Tutorial

The first/middle/final specimens reuse `tutorialShell`, with all six progress dots, Skip, conditional Back and final Got it. The order is Mode, Save queue, Discard queue, Delete one, Reorder, Split/join. Fresh installs see all six; returning users can see the relevant unseen subset with pagination derived from that subset. Descriptions refer to mixed media as items rather than only photos, and queue edge instructions identify the actual start handle.

The 82% black scrim covers the available window; the rounded specimen boundary is a review frame, not a requirement for a small modal tutorial. Preview demos anchor to the preview; queue demos anchor to the queue. Demo gestures cannot mutate real queued media. Keep the text/navigation area scrollable at large text, with at least 48dp navigation targets and system insets respected. Block underlying pointer interactions **and** accessibility focus; move focus into the tutorial, expose the current step, then restore focus when dismissed. Skip remains reachable throughout. The separate visible gesture legend preserves centered two-arrow swipe/hold markers, actual handle edge swipes, focus, pinch, gap directions and deletion instructions. Tutorial discard links its contact cue to the actual right handle center; split/join arrows start in the actual expanded gap. Instructional arrows/dots belong to the tutorial only.

## Icons, colors and framing

## Known native gaps and validation boundary

The source audit found cancellation routed to queue completion; width-dependent/velocity-only queue release; divider hit regions overlapping thumbnails; no split accessibility actions; recording queue input/exit feedback still active; missing busy guard on reorder; and different tutorial/banner/state geometry. Native voice and video already use white shutter rings. This document specifies the desired behavior and does not claim those implementation gaps were fixed.

Regenerate standalone definitions and review artifacts after the shared SVG stabilizes, then request independent final review. Static XML/reference, geometry, contrast and render checks cannot establish native hit testing, TalkBack focus, font scaling, physical reach, system gesture arbitration, camera-output agreement or recording continuity. Remaining hardware/runtime checks belong to later implementation; they do not prevent correction of the obvious static component issues now.
